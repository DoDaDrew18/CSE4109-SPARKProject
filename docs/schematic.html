SPARK · What we're building, layer by layer

A retrospective study: we rebuild the respiratory-illness data exactly as it looked on past dates, then ask, for one date at a time, which signals (weather, wastewater, local news) line up with what ED visits turned out to be. We nowcast today's value; we do not forecast the future.

QuestionOn a given past date, do weather and local news help estimate this week's share of respiratory ED visits in a county, beyond the official numbers available that day?
One ruleFor date D, use only data published on or before D. The final (later-corrected) numbers are the answer key, never an input.
Scope1 home city (St. Louis) + 5 comparison cities. ~2 flu seasons (Apr 2024 →). 60–200 hand-labeled news articles. Demo in 1 week.
LAYER 1

Collect

"What raw data exists, and where do we get it?"

CDC · health

NSSP % of ED visits for COVID, flu, RSV per county per week, with revision history.
Delphi Epidata · no key

CDC · wastewater

NWSS viral level per sewer site per week (COVID, flu, RSV). Revised silently; honest vintages from Apr 2026.
data.cdc.gov atcp-73re · no key

NOAA · weather

NCEI daily summaries: max/min temp, precipitation. NCEI humidity stops at 2024, so dew point / humidity come from IEM ASOS (same airport sensors). One station per city.
no key

News

Web search + GDELT (raw GKG files) to find local articles around past flu peaks. 196 candidates, 82 from St. Louis.
text stays local

LAYER 4

Read the news

"Can an LLM label articles as well as we do?"

In

60–200 local articles around past peaks, St. Louis first.
→

Do

  • Humans label every article (2 people on the first 30 → agreement)
  • LLM labels the same articles; each label must cite a quote, else dropped
  • Label concern level 0–3 + illness, not open-ended "sentiment"
→

Out

Article label table + LLM vs. human agreement. If the LLM is worse than human-vs-human, we use human labels only.
